## Key Question 1: Which listed companies own and operate nuclear plants, and how much of their business is nuclear?

### Takeaway
Constellation (CEG) is by far the largest US nuclear owner (roughly 23 GW across stakes in 15 plants, and nuclear output of ~46.7 TWh in Q3 2026 alone); Vistra (VST), Talen (TLN), Duke, Dominion, NextEra, Entergy, Southern and PSEG each own roughly 2-9 GW. The pure-play "merchant nuclear" names investors trade on the AI-power theme are CEG, VST and TLN; the regulated utilities (DUK, SO, D, NEE, ETR, PEG) own nuclear but are diversified.

### Inferences
- The "investable nuclear operator" set splits into (a) merchant generators whose equity value is highly geared to power prices and hyperscaler contracting (CEG, VST, TLN), and (b) regulated utilities where nuclear is a rate-based asset and the data-center story is mostly about gas/transmission capex, with uprates as a secondary lever.
- Japan (Kansai, Kyushu) is a restart-driven earnings story: nuclear utilization directly lowers fuel costs, as both companies' results explicitly credit.
- KEPCO (Korea) shows that nuclear exposure does not insulate a state-run utility from fuel-cost and tariff politics; its Q3 2026 collapse was driven by fossil fuel costs despite high nuclear utilization.

### Gaps
- No single up-to-date (2026) table of owned nuclear MW by company was retrievable (NEI's owner database was blocked). Figures for Southern, Dominion, Entergy and Duke nuclear MW were not confirmed in this session.
- Nuclear share of earnings for the regulated utilities (DUK, SO, D, NEE, ETR, PEG) was not found; only operational metrics were.
- EDF's listing status (fully nationalized/delisted in 2023, per general knowledge) was not verified from a source in this session; investors should treat EDF as accessible only via its bonds.
- No Q3 2026 results were found for Kansai beyond the announcement date; its H1 FY2026 figures were not captured.

## Key Question 2: Hyperscaler/data center nuclear deals signed 2024-2026, status and economics

### Takeaway
Between March 2024 and November 2026, Microsoft, Amazon, Meta and Google signed 20-25 year nuclear PPAs totaling roughly 10 GW with CEG, VST, TLN, NEE, Southern/Georgia Power and Fortum. The trend in 2026 moved from co-located single-plant deals toward front-of-meter, multi-unit uprate-plus-existing-fleet packages (Google-CEG 3,590 MW; Meta-Vistra 2,609 MW; Amazon-CEG Calvert Cliffs 690 MW), with pricing almost never disclosed.

### Inferences
- Price terms are undisclosed in every deal found; the only economics are Talen's ~$18B/17-year revenue estimate (implying very roughly $70-80/MWh at 1,920 MW and ~90% output, our arithmetic, not a sourced figure) and Fortum's +1.4 pp RONA.

## Key Question 3: Plant restarts and uprates underway, with timelines

### Takeaway
Three US restarts are in motion: Palisades (Holtec, MI, ~800 MW) is loading fuel after an October 2026 fuel-assembly incident and has no firm restart date but a March 2027 supply obligation; Crane/TMI-1 (CEG, 835 MW) targets 2027 with NRC environmental review done and tariff waivers contested; Duane Arnold (NEE, 615 MW) targets Q4 2028-early 2029 backed by a $1.9B DOE loan. Uprates under contract total roughly 1.6 GW (CEG 890 + 190 + 30; VST 433; Georgia Power 96; PSEG 112).
