# Nuclear Energy Investment Theme: Policy, Regulatory and Demand Drivers (as of mid-November 2026)

Research note on the macro backdrop for nuclear equities. Method note: web search snippets only; direct page fetches (discoveryalert.com, carnegieendowment.org, ans.org, sec.gov, finance.yahoo.com) were blocked by the session's network allowlist, so figures below are taken from search-result extracts and should be checked against the primary documents where flagged. Dates are given for every event; items older than 2026 are explicitly dated.

## Key Question 1: US federal policy (executive orders, DOE loans, Westinghouse partnership, tax credits, DPA/DOD, HALEU)

### Takeaway
The May 23, 2025 executive orders set a 400 GW-by-2050 target and forced the NRC into a wholesale rule rewrite; by November 2026 the NRC has finalized Part 53 and some rules but is openly slipping past the EO's 18-month (Nov 23, 2026) deadline on others. Money has followed: a $17.5B conditional DOE loan commitment for up to 10 AP1000s (June 2026), a $4.2B Vistra uprate commitment, a $1.9B Duane Arnold restart loan (closed Sept 2026), a non-binding $120B US-Korea framework for 8 large reactors (Sept 30, 2026), and a ~$2.2B Army microreactor program (Aug 2026). The 45U and nuclear-eligible 45Y/48E credits survived the 2025 budget law. Nearly all large-reactor commitments remain conditional or non-binding.

### Cited Findings

**The four executive orders (May 23, 2025) and targets**
- The May 23, 2025 orders set a goal of growing US nuclear capacity from about 100 GW to 400 GW by 2050; EO 14300 covers NRC reform and EO 14301 covers DOE reactor testing; other orders cover national-security deployment and fuel-cycle expansion — [K&L Gates](https://www.klgates.com/President-Trump-Issues-Sweeping-Executive-Orders-Targeting-Nuclear-Regulation-6-5-2025); [McGuireWoods](https://www.mcguirewoods.com/client-resources/alerts/2025/5/executive-orders-seek-resurgence-of-nuclear-power-in-u-s/); [Sullivan & Cromwell memo](https://www.sullcrom.com/SullivanCromwell/_Assets/PDFs/Memos/Four-New-Executive-Orders-Promoting-Nuclear-Energy.pdf); [White House EO text via govinfo](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/DCPD-202500633/pdf/DCPD-202500633.pdf)
- EO 14300 required NRC proposed rules within nine months (Feb 2026) and final rules within eighteen months; Sullivan & Cromwell dates the final-rule deadline at November 23, 2026 — [K&L Gates](https://www.klgates.com/President-Trump-Issues-Sweeping-Executive-Orders-Targeting-Nuclear-Regulation-6-5-2025); [Sullivan & Cromwell](https://www.sullcrom.com/SullivanCromwell/_Assets/PDFs/Memos/Four-New-Executive-Orders-Promoting-Nuclear-Energy.pdf)
- The DOE reactor pilot program under the EOs targeted criticality of at least three test reactors by July 4, 2026 — [K&L Gates](https://www.klgates.com/President-Trump-Issues-Sweeping-Executive-Orders-Targeting-Nuclear-Regulation-6-5-2025); [Clean Air Task Force on EO 14300](https://www.catf.us/resource/actions-address-executive-order-14300-reforming-nuclear-regulatory-commission/)
- Oklo's Groves test reactor reached first criticality on August 5, 2026 (i.e., after the July 4 goal) — [Motley Fool, Aug 27, 2026](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/08/27/oklo-and-nuscale-power-have-shed-a-combined-853-th/)

**NRC reform implementation status**
- A May 2026 secondary source says the NRC had 33 active rulemakings under way in response to EO 14300 — [Discovery Alert (secondary, unverified against NRC records)](https://discoveryalert.com/trump-nuclear-executive-orders-us-energy-policy-2026/)
- POWER reports the NRC finalized Part 53 (the risk-informed, technology-inclusive licensing framework for advanced reactors) in March 2026, about 21 months ahead of the NEIMA congressional deadline — [POWER Magazine](https://www.powermag.com/how-trumps-eo-14300-is-reshaping-nrc-nuclear-licensing-and-regulation/)

- A Federal Register final rule dated November 3, 2026 streamlines NRC contested adjudications and cites EO 14300 as its basis — [Federal Register, Nov 3, 2026](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/full_text/xml/2026/11/03/2026-22443.xml)
- The Nuclear Innovation Alliance maintains an "Expected NRC EO 14300 Rulemaking Timeline" (versions dated Feb 27 and Mar 13, 2026) — [NIA timeline PDF](https://nuclearinnovationalliance.org/sites/default/files/2026-03/Public%20Rule%20timeline%203-13.pdf)
- NRC construction permits issued in 2026 include: TerraPower's Kemmerer Unit 1 (Natrium, Wyoming), described as approval of construction of a new commercial reactor; and a construction permit to TVA for a BWRX-300 at Clinch River, Tennessee in September 2026, described as the first US permit for that design — [Core Insights Review (secondary)](https://www.coradvisors.net/2026/08/small-modular-reactor-nuclear-power-data-centers-2026.html); [Noreva US Nuclear Outlook 2026 (secondary)](https://noreva.ai/us-nuclear-outlook-2026-new-build-restarts-ratepayers/)

- September 30, 2026: US Department of Commerce and South Korea announced a non-binding framework under which Korea commits up to $120 billion of investment to help finance eight large reactors on US federal sites: six AP1000s and two APR1400s (the latter described as built on Westinghouse technology); Korean trade ministry materials describe a first phase of two AP1000s with APR1400s in a second phase — [World Nuclear News](https://www.world-nuclear-news.org/articles/usa-south-korea-targeting-six-ap1000-two-apr1400-units); [Brookfield/Westinghouse release via GlobeNewswire, Sep 30, 2026](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/09/30/3372473/0/en/u-s-korea-framework-advances-deployment-of-westinghouse-nuclear-technology-in-the-united-states.html); [UPI](https://www.upi.com/Top_News/World-News/2026/09/27/apr1400-ap1000-united-states-nuclear-power/7301790551653/)

**Tax credits after the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (signed July 2025)**
- The 45U zero-emission nuclear production credit "remained unscathed"; OBBBA added an energy-community bonus for nuclear communities — [Steptoe](https://www.steptoe.com/en/news-publications/the-one-big-beautiful-bill-impact-on-the-iras-clean-energy-tax-credits.html)
- Final bill made no change to the 45U credit amount or expiration date but imposed foreign-entity-of-concern (FEOC) restrictions; Taxpayers for Common Sense describes a hard cutoff at end-2031, whereas Sidley's summary describes 45U as applying to electricity sold before 2033 (sources conflict on the end year; check statute) — [Taxpayers for Common Sense](https://www.taxpayer.net/energy-natural-resources/energy-tax-provisions-in-the-one-big-beautiful-bill/); [Sidley](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2025/07/the-one-big-beautiful-bill-act-navigating-the-new-energy-landscape)
- Under the final law, advanced nuclear facilities and nuclear expansions that begin construction before January 1, 2029 remain eligible for the 45Y PTC or 48E ITC (while wind/solar were cut back), plus a 10% nuclear "energy community" bonus under 45Y — [Clifford Chance briefing](https://www.cliffordchance.com/content/dam/cliffordchance/briefings/2025/07/one-big-beautiful-bill-act-impacts-on-tax-credits-available-for-nuclear-energy-under-sections-45u-and-45y.pdf); [Steptoe](https://www.steptoe.com/en/news-publications/the-one-big-beautiful-bill-impact-on-the-iras-clean-energy-tax-credits.html)
- Credit transferability was preserved for nuclear under 45U, 45Y and 48E — [Taxpayers for Common Sense](https://www.taxpayer.net/energy-natural-resources/energy-tax-provisions-in-the-one-big-beautiful-bill/)

**HALEU and enrichment programs**

### Inferences
- The policy stack is now broad (EO targets, licensing reform, DOE debt, tax credits, DOD demand, fuel funding), but the large-reactor "10 under construction by 2030" goal still rests on conditional loan commitments and a non-binding Korean framework; no source found confirms a firm, financed AP1000 order with a named US utility site as of mid-November 2026.
- The NRC appears to be hitting advanced-reactor milestones (Part 53, Kemmerer, Clinch River permits) faster than the wholesale rewrite of legacy rules, several of which are now scheduled into 2027, so the "NRC reform" catalyst is partially delivered and partially pending.
- 45U's survival materially supports existing-fleet owners (Constellation, Vistra, PSEG, Talen) through at least 2031; the 2029 begin-construction window for 45Y/48E creates a start-construction deadline incentive for new builds.

## Key Question 2: Demand — AI data centers, PJM capacity prices, hyperscaler nuclear commitments

### Takeaway
Load forecasts keep rising (EPRI Feb 2026: data centers 9-17% of US electricity by 2030; Grid Strategies: 166 GW of US peak growth by 2030, ~90 GW tied to data centers, which it says is likely ~25 GW overstated), PJM has cleared at its price cap for three straight auctions with record-low reserve margins, and hyperscalers have announced roughly 9.8-10 GW of nuclear commitments, led by Meta's 6.6 GW (Jan 2026). Most new-build contracted nuclear is 2030+ and mostly undisclosed in price; the contracted share of data-center load is small relative to the forecast GW.

### Cited Findings

**Demand forecasts**
- IEA (April 2026 update, as cited by secondary sources): global data center electricity use ~485 TWh in 2025 rising to ~950 TWh by 2030; IEA's "Energy and AI" special report notes nuclear "also contributes" to meeting data center demand — [IEA Energy and AI report](https://iea.blob.core.windows.net/assets/de9dea13-b07d-42c5-a398-d1b3ae17d866/EnergyandAI.pdf); [dev/sustainability (secondary)](https://www.devsustainability.com/p/ai-data-center-energy-in-2026)
- EPRI "Powering Intelligence 2026" (Feb 25, 2026) raised US data center projections by ~60% vs its 2024 estimates: data centers 9%-17% of US electricity by 2030, low-to-high range ~384 TWh to ~793 TWh; AI workloads estimated at 15-25% of data center electricity today; EPRI modeling says hourly carbon-free matching favors renewables/batteries with new nuclear "where feasible" — [EPRI Powering Intelligence executive summary](https://powering-intelligence.epri.com/executive-summary.html)
- Grid Strategies 2025 National Load Growth Report: 166 GW of US peak load growth by 2030, ~90 GW (~55%) tied to data centers; the firm itself says utility forecasts of 90 GW data center load by 2030 are "likely overstated" and market analysis points closer to 65 GW (i.e., ~25 GW or 40% overestimate) — [Grid Strategies 2025 report PDF](https://gridstrategiesllc.com/wp-content/uploads/Grid-Strategies-National-Load-Growth-Report-2025.pdf); [Latitude Media](https://www.latitudemedia.com/news/one-us-load-growth-estimate-quadrupled-in-just-two-years/)

- Goldman Sachs Research forecasts US data center power demand of 31 GW (2025), 41 GW (2026), 66 GW (2027) — reported in [Data Centre Digest](https://datacentredigest.com/from-31-to-66-gigawatts-inside-the-power-demand-curve-that-will-reshape-the-us-grid-by-2027/)

## Key Question 4: Headwinds and risks

### Takeaway
The thesis faces a documented cost-overrun record (Vogtle ~$36.8bn/11 years; Hinkley Point C >= £49bn), a fuel-cycle bottleneck (Russia ~43-44% of global enrichment, US waiver authority ends Jan 1, 2028, conversion and HALEU tightest), slipping timelines (Palisades fuel-load pause, Duane Arnold to 2029, Janus to 2030+), rate sensitivity (nuclear stocks sold off on late-Oct 2026 Fed-tightening fears), and valuation drawdowns of roughly 50-85% in SMR developers during 2026, alongside market-monitor warnings that data-center load forecasts are overstated.
