# Nuclear Stocks Derate While Fundamentals Strengthen

The defining feature of the publicly traded nuclear landscape in mid-November 2026 is a divergence: the underlying fundamentals have never looked stronger, while the equities are in a bear market. The long-term uranium price set an all-time record of roughly **US$96-99/lb** in August-October 2026 ([Sprott](https://sprott.com/insights/record-term-prices-mark-a-new-phase-for-uranium/); [Crux Investor](https://www.cruxinvestor.com/posts/record-us-99-uranium-contract-price-favors-miners-with-supply-left-to-sell)), hyperscalers have signed roughly **10 GW** of 20-25 year nuclear power purchase agreements ([Nuclear News Network](https://www.nuclearnewsnetwork.com/news/nuclear-power-data-centers-explained)), the NRC issued the first three commercial advanced-reactor construction permits in US history during 2026, and the merchant operators Constellation, Vistra and Talen all reported record third quarters. Yet the VanEck Uranium and Nuclear ETF (NLR) is down **28.2% year-to-date** ([VanEck](https://www.vaneck.com/us/en/investments/uranium-nuclear-energy-etf-nlr/)), the Global X Uranium ETF (URA) roughly 28%, pre-revenue SMR developers NuScale and Oklo are down **61-62%**, and even Constellation and Vistra are 15-25% lower over a year. The investable universe sorts into four tiers with sharply different risk profiles: cash-generating plant operators (CEG, VST, TLN plus regulated utilities), uranium miners and fuel-cycle companies (Cameco, Kazatomprom, Centrus and a long tail of developers), pre-revenue SMR developers that collectively hold more than US$8 billion of cash but no binding reactor orders, and component suppliers (BWXT, Mirion, Curtiss-Wright, GE Vernova) that are the only segment with growing current earnings tied to nuclear. The 2026 selloff was driven by dilution, an inflation and rate shock tied to the Iran war, an unwind of the AI-power trade and a post-midterm political scare in Texas, not by any deterioration in uranium prices or policy. Bulls (Sprott, Wedbush, Morgan Stanley on Constellation, ARK) argue this is a mispricing; bears (Citi, Truist, short sellers who booked roughly US$2 billion of profits) argue the speculative end of the theme is still working through a reckoning on dilution, timelines and the absence of priced, binding orders.

## Merchant operators own the near-term demand story

The only companies that can sell nuclear electrons to data centers this decade are the ones that already own reactors, and the market has concentrated the AI-power theme in three merchant generators. Constellation is by far the largest US nuclear owner with stakes in **15 plants and about 23 GW** of nuclear capacity ([Thunder Said Energy](https://thundersaidenergy.com/downloads/us-nuclear-generation-by-company-by-facility/)); after closing its roughly **US$22 billion** acquisition of Calpine on January 7, 2026 it operates about 55 GW in total, so nuclear is well under half of its megawatts but remains the dominant earnings driver ([TipRanks](https://www.tipranks.com/news/company-announcements/constellation-energy-completes-strategic-acquisition-of-calpine)). Vistra runs Comanche Peak plus the roughly 4,000 MW Energy Harbor fleet in Ohio and Pennsylvania acquired in 2024 ([Vistra 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001692819/000114036126042013/ef20084950_ex99-1.htm)), and Talen's single nuclear asset is Susquehanna in Pennsylvania. Regulated utilities with sizable fleets (Duke, Southern, Dominion, NextEra, Entergy, PSEG) own nuclear as rate-based assets, but their data-center growth story is mostly about gas and transmission capex, with uprates as a secondary lever. Abroad, Fortum derived about **53%** of its Nordic output from nuclear in 2025 ([Green Stocks Research](https://greenstocksresearch.com/nuclear-power-stocks/)), Japan's Kansai and Kyushu are restart-driven earnings stories (Kyushu's ordinary profit rose **45.9%** in the April-June 2026 quarter on higher nuclear utilization, per [BigGo Finance](https://finance.biggo.com/news/ir_9508.T_20260731_c06ee56d7c22)), and Korea's KEPCO demonstrates that nuclear exposure does not insulate a state utility from fuel costs and tariff politics: its Q3 2026 operating income collapsed to **KRW 1.64 trillion from KRW 5.65 trillion** on higher LNG and coal costs ([TipRanks/KEPCO](https://www.tipranks.com/news/company-announcements/kepco-announces-unaudited-third-quarter-2026-results)).

The hyperscaler contracting wave is the reason these stocks re-rated in 2024-25, and 2026 saw it shift decisively from co-located single-plant deals toward front-of-meter, multi-unit packages that pay for uprates and existing output. Constellation now holds contracts with all four US hyperscalers; Vistra has Amazon and Meta; Talen has Amazon only, with roughly 300 MW of Susquehanna left to sell ([Talen Q3 2026 presentation](https://ir.talenenergy.com/static-files/06224f65-8b56-4726-8a3c-0a4fc6aa1634)). Skeptics note that roughly **three-quarters** of the Google-Constellation 3,590 MW package pays for reactors already running ([Global Data Center Hub](https://www.globaldatacenterhub.com/p/google-signed-for-3590-mw-of-nuclear)), and that Meta and Microsoft fund extensions and restarts while Amazon and Alphabet lean more on SMRs ([Carnegie Endowment](https://carnegieendowment.org/research/2026/06/beyond-the-hype-assessing-hyperscaler-nuclear-commitments-against-us-energy-realities)). The only economic data point in the public record is Talen's management estimate of about US$18 billion of revenue over the life of the Amazon contract, and Fortum's 1.4 percentage-point RONA uplift.

New megawatts are coming mainly from uprates, not new plants. Contracted uprates total roughly **1.6 GW** (Constellation 890 + 190 + 30 MW, Vistra 433 MW, Georgia Power 96 MW, PSEG's 112 MW share of a Salem uprate), exceeding the three US restarts in project count though not in megawatts ([Data Center Frontier](https://www.datacenterfrontier.com/energy/article/55410654/big-techs-next-nuclear-bet-more-power-from-reactors-already-running)). Of the restarts, Palisades (Holtec, ~800 MW) began fuel loading August 30, 2026, paused after a fuel assembly tipped in late September, retrieved it on October 22 and has no firm restart date against a March 2027 supply obligation ([ANS](https://www.ans.org/news/2026-09-25/article-8436/incident-pauses-palisades-fuel-loading-plant-connected-to-switchyard/); [Michigan Public](https://www.michiganpublic.org/environment-climate-change/2026-10-22/palisades-nuclear-plant-fuel-assembly)). Crane received its NRC environmental finding in September 2026 and targets 2027, but PJM's market monitor opposed Constellation's tariff waivers and one analysis warns grid connection could slip to 2031 ([Nuclear News Network](https://www.nuclearnewsnetwork.com/industry-trackers/nuclear-plant-restarts/crane-clean-energy-center); [Sahm Capital](https://www.sahmcapital.com/news/content/constellation-energy-reactor-restart-with-microsoft-raises-timing-and-earnings-questions-2026-03-27)). Duane Arnold has slipped to early 2029 ([ANS](https://www.ans.org/news/2026-01-28/article-7715/nextera-considers-new-nuclear-progresses-on-restart/)).

Third-quarter 2026 results were records across the merchant trio. Constellation posted adjusted EPS of **US$5.06 versus US$3.04** a year earlier and raised 2026 guidance to US$12.50-12.80 from US$11.50-12.50 ([Constellation](https://www.constellationenergy.com/news/2026/11/constellation-reports-third-quarter-2026-results.html)); Vistra reported record adjusted EBITDA of **US$2,378 million**, up more than 50%, raised its 2026 range to US$7.1-7.5 billion and laid out a US$24 free-cash-flow-per-share vision for 2030 ([Vistra](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/vistra-reports-third-quarter-2026-results-302931798.html)); Talen introduced 2027 guidance of **US$2.5-2.85 billion** adjusted EBITDA alongside a US$1.5 billion accelerated share repurchase and a US$3.0 billion total buyback authorization ([Talen 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001622536/000162253626000084/a20261103q32026earningsrel.htm)). Notably, management attributed the guidance raises mainly to commercial and hedging outperformance in volatile power markets; most contracted nuclear revenue begins in 2027 (Clinton, Comanche Peak, Crane) or later. Dealmaking by these operators in 2026 was entirely about adding gas around the nuclear core: Vistra closed the roughly US$4 billion Cogentrix purchase (5.5 GW of gas) on October 30, 2026 ([Vistra 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001692819/000114036126042013/ef20084950_ex99-1.htm)) and Constellation agreed to buy a 609 MW Rhode Island gas plant for US$715 million ([Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/constellation-energy-purchase-609-mw-rhode-island-gas-plant-shell/830270/)). No listed nuclear plant changed hands in 2026; the asset-level "M&A" has taken the form of long-dated PPAs and uprate financings.

## Uranium term prices hit records while miners stumble on volumes

The uranium market is in a historically unusual configuration: the term price has broken out to all-time highs while spot lags and equities have fallen. Spot U3O8 sat at roughly **US$90-92/lb** in mid-November 2026 ([FNArena](https://fnarena.com/2026/11/10/uranium-week-u308s-blockchain-tokenisation/); [Public.com](https://public.com/invest/uranium-price)), below the January 2026 spike that sources place somewhere between US$94 and US$101 ([Crux Investor](https://www.cruxinvestor.com/posts/q1-uranium-recap-how-governments-are-deciding-who-secures-uranium); [Investing News](https://investingnews.com/uranium-forecast/)) but far above the March 2025 low of about US$63 ([Investing News](https://investingnews.com/uranium-price-update/)). TradeTech's long-term indicator reached **US$99.00/lb on October 31, 2026**, the highest since its series began in 1996 ([Crux Investor](https://www.cruxinvestor.com/posts/record-us-99-uranium-contract-price-favors-miners-with-supply-left-to-sell)), and Sprott flagged the August 2026 term print of US$96 as surpassing the previous cycle high of nearly two decades earlier ([Sprott](https://sprott.com/insights/record-term-prices-mark-a-new-phase-for-uranium/)). Paradoxically, this record is happening with thin contracting: utilities had signed only about 37-38 Mlb of term volume by August 2026, which Sprott says puts the industry on track for a **14th consecutive year of below-replacement contracting** against a theoretical replacement rate of about 150 Mlb per year ([Sprott](https://sprott.com/insights/on-the-cusp-of-a-new-contracting-cycle/); [Sprott Dec 2025](https://www.mining.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/Sprott-Uranium-Report-Dec-2025.pdf)). US utilities' realized prices still reflect legacy contracts: the EIA's 2025 weighted average was just **US$58.46/lb** with 186 Mlb of unfilled requirements through 2035 ([EIA](https://www.eia.gov/uranium/marketing/pdf/umar.pdf)). On the supply side, the World Nuclear Association's 2025 fuel report projects reactor requirements rising from about 69,000 tU in 2025 to over 150,000 tU by 2040 in its reference case, with output from existing mines roughly halving between 2030 and 2040 ([World Nuclear Association](https://world-nuclear.org/our-association/publications/global-trends-reports/world-nuclear-fuel-report-2025)).

The record price has not translated into producer earnings because 2026 has been a year of disappointing volumes. Cameco's Q3 2026 revenue was flat at **C$619 million**, gross profit fell 28% to C$122 million and the company posted an adjusted net loss of C$2 million, blaming lower Westinghouse equity earnings, lower sales volumes and higher unit costs ([Cameco 6-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001009001/000119312526439858/d106072dex991.htm)). Production fell about 20% to 3.5 Mlb on an extended Key Lake shutdown, 2026 guidance was narrowed down to 19.5-20.5 Mlb, and Cameco said it has no plans to run McArthur River/Key Lake at full licensed capacity ([Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/company-news/cameco-narrows-2026-production-guidance-reports-lower-earnings-93CH-4986807)). Its realized price of about **US$65.78/lb** against spot near US$90 illustrates how a 230 Mlb legacy contract book dampens leverage to the commodity ([Uranium Unleashed](https://uraniumunleashed.substack.com/p/cameco-realized-us6578lb-in-q3-spot); [Cameco 6-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1009001/000119312526049832/d34107dex993.htm)). Cameco's 49% Westinghouse stake is now a swing factor: its share of Westinghouse was a US$24 million net loss in Q3 and nine-month EBITDA share fell to US$427 million from US$569 million because 2025 included a Dukovany milestone payment ([StreetInsider](https://www.streetinsider.com/Corporate+News/Cameco+Q3+2026+earnings+fall+on+lower+Westinghouse+income,+higher+costs/27261399.html)). Shares closed at US$76.91 on October 30 after the miss, about 43% below the 52-week high of US$135.24, and were near US$70 by mid-November ([Admiral Markets](https://admiralmarkets.com/education/articles/shares/uranium-stocks-to-watch); [Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/stock-market/market-sectors/energy/nuclear/)).

Kazatomprom, the world's largest producer, grew first-half 2026 output **9% to 13,291 tU** and lifted its realized price 16% to US$67.88/lb, but net profit fell 9% on FX and financing costs, and the company's sulfuric acid plant has slipped to between Q3 2027 and Q1 2028, with analysts estimating a Russian acid export ban could cut 2027 output by about 3 Mlb ([Kazatomprom](https://www.kazatomprom.kz/en/media/view/Kazatomprom%20announces%201H2026%20Financial%20Results); [Discovery Alert](https://discoveryalert.com/analysis/kazatomprom-sulfuric-acid-uranium-supply-september-2026/)). The US in-situ producers are small but finally profitable at the gross-margin line: Energy Fuels sold 320,000 lb at a weighted **US$88.90/lb** with a ~63% uranium gross margin ([Energy Fuels](https://finance.yahoo.com/energy/articles/energy-fuels-announces-q3-2026-211500677.html)), and Uranium Energy Corp realized US$93.13/lb in fiscal 2026 with US$753 million of liquid assets and no debt ([UEC](https://www.nasdaq.com/press-release/uranium-energy-corp-reports-fiscal-2026-results-2026-09-29)), though UEC shares were still about 62% below their 52-week high in late October ([Admiral Markets](https://admiralmarkets.com/education/articles/shares/uranium-stocks-to-watch)). Among Australians, Paladin printed a record realized US$79.40/lb and on November 12 raised **A$525 million** at a 10.3% discount, mostly to fund Patterson Lake South in Saskatchewan for 2031 first production ([Discovery Alert](https://discoveryalert.com/asx-stock-news/energy/pdn-paladin-energy-pls-equity-raising-november-2026/)), while Boss Energy fell 14% after resetting Honeymoon expectations ([Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/transcripts/earnings-call-transcript-boss-energy-falls-14-after-h2-2026-update-93CH-4878121)).

Financing is the dividing line among developers. NexGen began construction of Rook I (up to 30 Mlb/yr) on June 8, 2026, holds about C$822 million of cash but states it will need more financing against a C$2.2 billion capex estimate, targeting first production in late 2029 or 2030 ([NexGen 6-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001698535/000106299326005850/exhibit99-2.htm); [Sharecafe](https://www.sharecafe.com.au/2026/11/10/nexgen-energy-asx-nxg-narrows-loss-as-rook-i-construction-accelerates/)). Denison's Phoenix ISR mine was **24% complete** after its first quarter of full-scale construction, targeting first production in the second half of 2028 ([Denison](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/denison-reports-financial-and-operational-results-for-q3-2026-highlighted-by-24-total-completion-after-first-quarter-of-full-scale-construction-at-phoenix-isr-uranium-mine-302929901.html)). At the other end, Deep Yellow dropped its Q4 2026 FID target for Tumas citing equity market conditions, with shares down about 61% over a year ([Discovery Alert](https://discoveryalert.com/news/tumas-uranium-fid-deep-yellow-october-2026/)), and Global Atomic's Dasa project in Niger depends on a US$414 million DFC loan whose disbursement is conditioned on an export route and government agreements ([Global Atomic](https://www.newswire.ca/news-releases/global-atomic-announces-q3-2026-results-890870350.html)). Sprott's October 30 assessment was blunt: "liquidity shocks, risk-off sentiment heavily impacting uranium equities" even as term prices set records ([Mining Weekly](https://www.miningweekly.com/article/liquidity-shocks-risk-off-sentiment-heavily-impacting-uranium-equities-sprott-reports-2026-10-30)).

### Enrichment is the fuel cycle's tightest link and its most volatile stock

The enrichment story moved from legislation to money in 2026. In January DOE awarded **US$900 million each** to Centrus, Orano and newcomer General Matter under US$2.7 billion of task orders ([World Nuclear News](https://www.world-nuclear-news.org/articles/doe-awards-usd27-billion-to-strengthen-us-uranium-enrichment)); General Matter's US$1.5 billion Paducah licence application was accepted by the NRC in October 2026 ([Arms Control Association](https://www.armscontrol.org/act/2026-11/news/us-china-expand-uranium-enrichment)); Orano's roughly US$5 billion Project IKE at Oak Ridge and Urenco's HALEU expansion all target production in the early 2030s ([World Nuclear News](https://www.world-nuclear-news.org/articles/doe-awards-usd27-billion-to-strengthen-us-uranium-enrichment)). That timing matters because the waiver regime under the 2024 Russian LEU import ban expires **January 1, 2028**, and Russia supplies roughly 43-44% of global enrichment ([DOE](https://www.energy.gov/node/4841498); [LLNL](https://cgsr.llnl.gov/sites/cgsr/files/2025-09/Sobalvarro%20Sarah%20-%20Final%20Draft%20on%20Decoupling%20from%20Russian%20Uranium_0.pdf)). Western capacity will not materially expand before that cliff.

Centrus Energy, the only listed Western enricher, embodies the tension. Its Q3 2026 revenue fell 26% to **US$55.5 million** and it posted an adjusted loss of US$9.4 million, yet it holds about US$5.1 billion of definitive LEU and HALEU sales commitments, has begun shipping new centrifuges to Piketon ahead of schedule under a Fluor EPC contract, and reaffirmed 2026 revenue guidance of US$450-500 million ([Centrus](https://investors.centrusenergy.com/news-releases/news-release-details/centrus-reports-third-quarter-2026-results)). Two overhangs weigh on it: DOE's FY2027 budget request includes no funding for the HALEU Operation Contract, and "well over one-half" of the LEU Centrus expects to deliver through 2027 is sourced from Russia's TENEX under waivers ([Centrus 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001065059/000162828026053863/leu-20260630.htm)). The stock closed at US$103.29 on October 30, **73.8% below its 52-week high** after a more than 400% one-year climb, and its US$700 million zero-coupon converts due 2032 convert at about US$229.62, more than double the share price ([Admiral Markets](https://admiralmarkets.com/education/articles/shares/uranium-stocks-to-watch); [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/company-news/centrus-energy-prices-700-million-convertible-senior-notes-93CH-4190638)).

## SMR developers hold billions in cash but no binding orders

2026 was simultaneously the best year on record for SMR regulatory milestones and the worst for SMR stocks. The NRC issued the first US construction permit for a commercial non-light-water reactor to TerraPower's Natrium on March 4, 2026 ([DOE](https://www.energy.gov/ne/articles/nrc-issues-construction-permit-terrapowers-natrium-advanced-reactor)), the first BWRX-300 permit to TVA's Clinch River on September 28 after a roughly 14-month review ([World Nuclear News](https://www.world-nuclear-news.org/articles/first-construction-permit-issued-for-bwrx300-smr-in-us)), and the Dow/X-energy Long Mott permit in early November "over a month ahead of schedule" ([ANS](https://www.ans.org/news/article-8573/nrc-issues-construction-permit-for-dowxenergy-long-mott-project/)). Kairos broke ground on Hermes 2, the first power-producing advanced plant tied to a Google offtake, on April 17 ([Kairos](https://www.kairospower.com/updates/kairos-power-breaks-ground-on-hermes-2-demonstration-plant)). The optional Part 53 risk-informed framework took effect April 29, 2026, more than a year ahead of its statutory deadline ([ANS](https://www.ans.org/news/2026-03-26/article-7881/nrc-unveils-part-53-final-rule/)). Licensing, in other words, is no longer the binding constraint. Financing and firm offtake are, and that is what the market is pricing.

The listed pure plays are all pre-revenue or nearly so, and each has traded down to a fraction of its late-2025 peak. NuScale, the only developer with an NRC-certified design, reported Q3 revenue of **US$0.3 million** against US$65 million of quarterly operating expenses, holds US$1.9 billion of cash and investments, and still has no module order; management stressed that even a TVA power purchase agreement with its commercial partner ENTRA1 would not itself constitute an order ([NuScale 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001822966/000182296626000089/pressreleasenuscale2026q.htm)). Its Class A share count rose from 318.5 million to 410.4 million in the first half of 2026 through a US$984.5 million ATM, a new US$750 million ATM was signed in August, and Fluor fully exited, collecting **US$2.43 billion** on a US$570 million investment ([StockTitan](https://www.stocktitan.net/sec-filings/SMR/10-q-nuscale-power-corp-quarterly-earnings-report-1337e66b3c9a.html); [Fluor 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001124198/000112419826000078/flr-20260930.htm)). The stock closed at US$4.82 on November 12, down about **61%** in 2026 ([24/7 Wall St](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/11/09/nuscale-is-still-down-61-in-2026-did-today-mark-the-bottom-for-smr-stock/)).

Oklo has the largest war chest, **US$3.13 billion** of cash and marketable securities at September 30 after raising US$2.254 billion net through ATMs in nine months, against a Q3 net loss of US$41.7 million and 2026 operating cash use guided at US$120-150 million ([Oklo 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001849056/000162828026071319/oklo-20260930.htm)). It is pursuing DOE authorization rather than NRC licensing for its first Aurora unit at Idaho National Laboratory, received DOE's Preliminary Documented Safety Analysis approval in June 2026, and has structural steel going up, but its 10-Q concedes "it is uncertain when, if at all, we will obtain NRC approvals" ([Oklo](https://oklo.com/newsroom/news-details/2026/U-S--Department-of-Energy-Approves-Preliminary-Documented-Safety-Analysis-for-Aurora-Powerhouse-at-Idaho-National-Laboratory/default.aspx); [Oklo 10-Q Q2](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001849056/000162828026054571/oklo-20260630.htm)). Its roughly 14 GW pipeline is anchored by a non-binding 12 GW agreement with Switch ([Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/oklo-aurora-smr-advanced-nuclear-reactor-supply-agreement-data-center-developer-switch/735933/)), plus a share of Meta's January 2026 commitment of up to 6.6 GW across Vistra, Oklo and TerraPower ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/01/09/meta-signs-nuclear-energy-deals-to-power-prometheus-ai-supercluster.html)). Shares closed at US$23.92 on November 13, down about 62% year-to-date, against a 52-week range of US$18.54-115.72 ([MarketBeat](https://www.marketbeat.com/instant-alerts/analyst-truist-financial-issues-pessimistic-forecast-for-oklo-nyse-oklo-stock-price-2026-11-13/); [StockAnalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/oklo/)).

X-energy, which priced the largest nuclear IPO on record at **US$23 on April 23, 2026** and closed its first day at US$29.20 ([IPOScoop](https://www.iposcoop.com/the-ipo-buzz-x-energy-xe-upsizes-ipo-prices-it-at-23-4-above-range/)), is the one developer with meaningful revenue: Q3 revenue and grant income of US$66.3 million, up 118%, mostly DOE-funded design work, with US$1.8 billion of cash and no debt ([X-energy 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0002088896/000119312526456633/xe-ex99_1.htm)). The Long Mott permit was a landmark, yet Dow has not committed and will weigh a preliminary investment decision in 2027 ([ANS](https://www.ans.org/news/article-8573/nrc-issues-construction-permit-for-dowxenergy-long-mott-project/)); the stock "barely reacted" and traded at US$11.08, about 52% below its IPO price ([MarketBeat](https://www.marketbeat.com/stocks/NASDAQ/XE/)). NANO Nuclear (about US$580 million of cash, KRONOS application accepted by the NRC in May 2026, shares down roughly 48% at US$11.11), Terrestrial Energy (US$276 million, no debt, a US$25 million buyback), Lightbridge (US$237 million) and Deep Fission (a cut-down US$40 million IPO in June) round out the listed set ([TradingView](https://www.tradingview.com/news/tradingview:d41b83e92237d:0-nano-nuclear-reports-q3-fy2026-results-nrc-accepts-kronos-cpa-ends-q3-with-580m-cash/); [GuruFocus](https://www.gurufocus.com/news/9196122/terrestrial-energy-inc-imsr-q3-2026-earnings-call-highlights-strong-276m-cash-position-25m-buyback-and-expanding-imsr-pipeline); [Renaissance Capital](https://www.renaissancecapital.com/IPO-Center/News/119911/Nuclear-tech-developer-Deep-Fission-prices-IPO-at-$16-the-low-end-of-the-do)). Fermi, the Texas AP1000-and-gas campus developer, was down 68% year-to-date before announcing on November 12 a US$200 million prepayment from an unnamed investment-grade hyperscaler for an initial 600 MW, which sent the stock up more than 20% in its best day ever ([Fermi 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0002071778/000121390026118351/ea031197701ex99-1.htm); [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/frmi-stock-clocks-best-day-222359238.html)). Holtec set terms for a roughly US$825 million Nasdaq IPO in September 2026 at an implied US$10.2 billion valuation ([Renaissance Capital](https://www.renaissancecapital.com/IPO-Center/News/121530/nuclear-plant-equipment-provider-holtec-nuclear-sets-terms-for-825-million)), though the research could not confirm whether it priced; a later ENR report says the IPO was shelved ([ENR](https://www.enr.com/articles/63675-holtec-advances-palisades-small-reactor-construction-shelves-planned-ipo)).

The first commercial SMR to reach construction has also produced the first public overrun. Ontario Power Generation acknowledged that Darlington BWRX-300 Unit 1 will exceed its **C$7.7 billion** budget and miss its October 2030 target, blaming GE Vernova Hitachi's first-of-a-kind engineering, with a revised estimate not due until mid-2027 ([World Nuclear News](https://www.world-nuclear-news.org/articles/opg-to-revise-timeline-and-cost-of-first-smr-unit)). This gives skeptics of every SMR schedule a concrete data point and is a direct headwind for TVA's willingness to commit at Clinch River, where no cost, start date or partner has been announced ([mgrid](https://mgrid.org/2026/10/01/nrc-clears-tva-to-build-a-300-mw-bwrx-300-at-clinch-river-with-no-cost-start-date-or-partner-deal/)).

### Suppliers are where nuclear revenue actually exists

The component and engineering suppliers are the only segment with current nuclear earnings and visible backlog growth. BWX Technologies reported Q3 revenue of **US$953 million** (up 10%) and a backlog of US$8.56 billion (up 15.6%), tightened 2026 non-GAAP EPS guidance to US$4.72-4.77, and won microreactor selections for the Army's Janus program ([BWXT 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001486957/000148695726000053/bwxt_93026xerexhibit991.htm)). Mirion's nuclear power orders rose **105%** excluding acquisitions and it booked US$54 million of year-to-date orders from 11 different SMR designers, the clearest hard-dollar evidence that developer cash is flowing into the supply chain ([Investing.com](https://uk.investing.com/news/stock-market-news/mirion-q3-2026-slides-nuclear-orders-surge-105-guidance-raised-93CH-4938618)). Curtiss-Wright raised 2026 guidance and still expects an AP1000 order (content potentially above US$150 million per unit) but keeps it out of guidance because timing depends on Westinghouse landing a utility order ([Yahoo Finance](https://au.finance.yahoo.com/news/cw-q3-2026-deep-dive-081336387.html)). GE Vernova's total backlog hit **US$197 billion** in Q3 but nuclear remains a sub-US$1 billion revenue line focused on the installed base, with management promising "many multiples" of that ([GEV](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1996810/000199681026000162/gevpressrelease3q26.htm); [POWER](https://www.powermag.com/blue-energy-ge-vernova-advance-gas-bridge-model-to-unlock-nuclear-finance/)). Outside the US, Rolls-Royce SMR signed its contract with Great British Energy-Nuclear in April 2026 to begin three 470 MW units at Wylfa and named Doosan Enerbility and Škoda JS as strategic suppliers ([ANS](https://www.ans.org/news/2026-04-15/article-7937/rollsroyce-gben-contract-kickstarts-uks-smr-plans-for-wylfa-site/); [World Nuclear News](https://www.world-nuclear-news.org/articles/skoda-js-doosan-enerbility-get-key-rolls-royce-smr-work)), while Hyundai E&C has accumulated roles on Westinghouse's Dutch AP1000s, up to eight TerraPower Natrium units and Fermi's Texas campus ([Seoul Economic Daily](https://en.sedaily.com/finance/2026/10/08/hyundai-ec-joins-design-phase-of-dutch-nuclear-plant-project); [UPI](https://www.upi.com/Top_News/World-News/2026/08/18/Hyundai-EC-TerraPower/4611787096819/)).

## Physical uranium trusts trade at a discount, sidelining the biggest spot buyer

The physical vehicles hold roughly 106 Mlb of uranium between them. The Sprott Physical Uranium Trust held about **81.7 Mlb** worth US$7.32 billion against a US$7.40 billion NAV at September 30, 2026 ([SPUT presentation](https://sprott.com/media/ttzpepac/sprott-physical-uranium-trust-investor-presentation.pdf)), and Yellow Cake plc held 24.37 Mlb valued at US$2.18 billion after taking delivery of about 1.16 Mlb from Kazatomprom in the quarter ([Yellow Cake](https://www.tradingview.com/news/reuters.com,2026-10-27:newsml_RSa5147Aa:0-reg-yellow-cake-plc-quarterly-operating-update/)). The critical development of 2026 is that SPUT has traded at a deepening discount to NAV, roughly 5.5% in May, 8-9% in July, 10% in September and **15.2% on November 15** (US$18.34 units versus US$21.62 NAV), which keeps its at-the-market program closed because it issues only at a premium ([Yellowcake Analytics](https://www.yellowcakeanalytics.com/sprott); [Uranium Edge](https://uranium-edge.com/reports/physical_uranium_trusts)). The single largest discretionary spot buyer of 2021-24 is therefore sidelined, which partly explains why spot lags term by US$6-9/lb. SPUT is also embedded in the equity ETFs, at 7.06% of URA and 17.90% of URNM ([MarketXLS](https://marketxls.com/etfs/ura/top10holdings); [Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/etfs/arcx/urnm/quote)), so ETF outflows and the trust discount reinforce each other.

The equity ETFs give the cleanest read on the sector's 2026 round trip. URA returned **67.4%** in calendar 2025 after a flat 2024 ([Global X prospectus](https://assets.globalxetfs.com/funds/documents/ura/prospectus-regulatory/Summary-Prospectus_URA.pdf); [Koalagains](https://koalagains.com/etfs/NYSEARCA/URA/performance-returns)), and NLR returned 55.9% ([VanEck prospectus](https://vaneck.onlineprospectus.net/VanEck/MOB_library/MOB_data/LIB_SummaryProspectus/NLRSUM/NLRSUM.pdf)). In 2026 URA was down 28.3% and URNM 31.7% as of November 3 ([MarketXLS](https://marketxls.com/etfs/vs/ura-vs-urnm)), and NLR was down 28.2% as of November 13 ([VanEck](https://www.vaneck.com/us/en/investments/uranium-nuclear-energy-etf-nlr/)). Almost all of the damage came in the second half: at mid-June URA was still up 6.5% year-to-date ([24/7 Wall St](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/06/16/ura-vs-urnm-which-uranium-etf-best-plays-the-nuclear-boom/)). Assets have followed prices and then some: NLR's net assets fell from about US$4.57 billion at March 31 to **US$2.90 billion** on November 13, a 37% decline that exceeds its price drop over the window and implies net outflows ([Fintel](https://fintel.io/s/us/nlr); [VanEck](https://www.vaneck.com/us/en/investments/uranium-nuclear-energy-etf-nlr/)). URA reported about US$4.7 billion of net assets with Cameco at 21.8% of the portfolio, followed by SPUT, NexGen, Oklo and UEC ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/URA/); [MarketXLS](https://marketxls.com/etfs/ura/top10holdings)). Smaller vehicles include Sprott's junior miners fund URNJ (US$313 million) and the Range Nuclear Renaissance ETF NUKZ (about US$705 million across roughly 50 holdings spanning developers, utilities, miners and fuel services) ([Yellowcake Analytics](https://www.yellowcakeanalytics.com/learn/best-uranium-etfs); [ETFdb](https://etfdb.com/nuclear-energy-content-hub/tax-loss-harvesting-start-with-nuclear-energy-etfs/)). Expense ratios run from 0.52% (NLR) to 0.80% (URNJ) ([FourPercentRule](https://www.fourpercentrule.com/etfs/guides/uranium)). Sprott Inc. told investors that uranium is now about US$10 billion of its US$57 billion AUM and that critical-materials products saw net inflows in 2026 even as precious metals saw outflows ([Motley Fool transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/11/04/sprott-sii-q3-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)).

## Policy support is broad, but every large-reactor commitment is still conditional

The policy stack behind the theme has never been wider. The May 23, 2025 executive orders set a target of **400 GW by 2050** from about 100 GW today and ordered the NRC to rewrite its rules within 18 months ([K&L Gates](https://www.klgates.com/President-Trump-Issues-Sweeping-Executive-Orders-Targeting-Nuclear-Regulation-6-5-2025)). By November 2026 the NRC had finalized Part 53, adopted generic 18-month review schedules, published a November 3, 2026 final rule streamlining contested hearings ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/03/2026-22443/streamlining-contested-adjudications-in-licensing-proceedings)) and issued three advanced-reactor permits, though several legacy-rule rewrites are now scheduled into 2027, past the EO's November 23, 2026 deadline ([POWER](https://www.powermag.com/how-trumps-eo-14300-is-reshaping-nrc-nuclear-licensing-and-regulation/)). DOE's reactor pilot program saw four test reactors reach criticality in 2026, beating its three-reactor goal ([DOE](https://www.energy.gov/articles/department-energy-celebrates-fourth-criticality-ahead-july-4th-goal)). The July 2025 budget law left the 45U production credit for existing plants "unscathed" and preserved 45Y/48E eligibility for nuclear projects that begin construction before January 1, 2029 while cutting wind and solar ([Steptoe](https://www.steptoe.com/en/news-publications/the-one-big-beautiful-bill-impact-on-the-iras-clean-energy-tax-credits.html); [Clifford Chance](https://www.cliffordchance.com/content/dam/cliffordchance/briefings/2025/07/one-big-beautiful-bill-act-impacts-on-tax-credits-available-for-nuclear-energy-under-sections-45u-and-45y.pdf)).

Money has followed, but almost all of it is conditional. DOE's renamed Office of Energy Dominance Financing issued a **US$17.5 billion** conditional commitment in June 2026 for up to 10 AP1000s at as many as five sites, requiring Westinghouse and a utility partner to each commit roughly US$500 million of equity per project upfront ([ANS](https://www.ans.org/news/2026-06-23/article-8142/doe-announces-175b-in-conditional-loans-for-ap1000-builds/)); the Union of Concerned Scientists called it "a drop in the bucket" against likely costs ([Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/doe-offers-175b-loans-help-build-10-large-nuclear-reactors/823633/)). The October 2025 US$80 billion US government-Westinghouse-Brookfield-Cameco partnership was supplemented on September 30, 2026 by a **non-binding** US-Korea framework for up to US$120 billion toward six AP1000s and two APR1400s, which Korea's industry minister said "does not mean that the nuclear power projects will immediately proceed" ([World Nuclear News](https://www.world-nuclear-news.org/articles/usa-south-korea-targeting-six-ap1000-two-apr1400-units); [UPI](https://www.upi.com/Top_News/World-News/2026/09/27/apr1400-ap1000-united-states-nuclear-power/7301790551653/)). No source in this research confirms a firm, financed AP1000 order with a named US utility site, and Curtiss-Wright's repeated deferral of its expected order corroborates that. Firmer commitments cluster around the existing fleet: a US$4.2 billion conditional DOE commitment for Vistra's 433 MW of uprates ([Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/doe-intends-to-loan-vistra-42b-for-nuclear-fleet-improvements/832236/)), closed loans of US$1.9 billion for Duane Arnold, US$1 billion for Crane and US$1.52 billion for Palisades ([Nuclear News Network](https://www.nuclearnewsnetwork.com/news/doe-nuclear-funding-explained)), and the Army's roughly US$2.2 billion Janus microreactor program, whose first power is realistically 2030 or later ([Defense One](https://www.defenseone.com/business/2026/08/army-selects-5-companies-build-nuclear-microreactors-bases-under-new-2-billion-deal/415653/); [National Defense Magazine](https://www.nationaldefensemagazine.org/articles/2026/8/31/small-nuclear-reactors-on-horizon-as-pentagon-programs-eye-2028-goal)).

On the demand side, the forecasts keep rising but the forecasters themselves warn of overstatement. EPRI's February 2026 update raised its estimate by about 60% to data centers consuming **9-17% of US electricity by 2030** ([EPRI](https://powering-intelligence.epri.com/executive-summary.html)); Grid Strategies sees 166 GW of US peak growth by 2030 with about 90 GW tied to data centers, but says that figure is "likely overstated" by roughly 25 GW ([Grid Strategies](https://gridstrategiesllc.com/wp-content/uploads/Grid-Strategies-National-Load-Growth-Report-2025.pdf)); Goldman Sachs projects US data center demand of 31 GW in 2025 rising to 66 GW in 2027 ([Data Centre Digest](https://datacentredigest.com/from-31-to-66-gigawatts-inside-the-power-demand-curve-that-will-reshape-the-us-grid-by-2027/)). The tightest market signal is PJM, where the 2028/29 capacity auction cleared at the **US$325/MW-day cap** with a record-low 14.7% reserve margin and 6,831 MW short of its reliability requirement; the uncapped price would have been roughly US$555/MW-day ([PJM](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/pjm-capacity-auction-procures-138-318-mw-of-generation-resources-as-work-continues-to-address-growing-electricity-demand-302825613.html); [Avalon Energy](https://avalonenergy.us/2026/07/pjm-2028-2029-base-residual-auction-clears-at-price-cap/)). PJM's independent market monitor concluded that some forecast data center load in that auction was "unverified and overstated" ([Monitoring Analytics](https://www.monitoringanalytics.com/reports/PJM_State_of_the_Market/2026/2026q3-som-pjm-sec1.pdf)), which creates two-sided risk for merchant nuclear: upside if caps lapse, downside if load is revised or regulators intervene. Of the roughly 10 GW of hyperscaler nuclear commitments, only the existing-plant PPAs deliver before 2029; the 4-5 GW of SMR new-build is 2030-plus.

Internationally, momentum is broad but uneven. China approved **eight more reactors** on July 31, 2026 (roughly 160-170 billion yuan) toward a 110 GW operating target by 2030 ([World Nuclear News](https://www.world-nuclear-news.org/articles/china-approves-construction-of-eight-more-reactors); [Caixin](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2026-08-01/beijing-clears-eight-new-reactors-as-china-accelerates-nuclear-buildout-102470294.html)), making it the dominant source of uranium demand growth. India's SHANTI Act (December 2025) opened the sector to private and 49% foreign capital ([The Asia Group](https://theasiagroup.com/india-opens-nuclear-sector-to-private-participation/)); Japan restarted Kashiwazaki-Kariwa 6 in January 2026 ([Nippon.com](https://www.nippon.com/en/articles/-/54875)); 14 EU states signed a Nuclear Alliance declaration in March 2026 as Sweden and Belgium reversed phase-outs ([Brussels Report](https://www.brusselsreport.eu/2026/09/11/the-nuclear-alliance-how-14-eu-states-are-rewriting-energy-policy/)). The setbacks are instructive: KHNP broke off construction-contract talks for the Czech Dukovany project on October 29, 2026 over a roughly 1.8 trillion won cost gap ([NucNet](https://www.nucnet.org/news/south-korea-s-khnp-breaks-off-construction-contract-talks-on-dukovany-reactor-project-10-5-2026); [Seoul Economic Daily](https://en.sedaily.com/finance/2026/11/09/dukovany-talks-collapse-over-25-percent-volume-demand-13)), a cautionary signal for the US-Korea framework; the World Bank has financed no project a year after lifting its ban ([ANS](https://www.ans.org/news/2026-07-09/article-8192/world-bank-and-iaea-partnership-at-one-year/)); and Hinkley Point C is now estimated at **at least £49 billion** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinkley_Point_C_nuclear_power_station)).

## 2026 performance: dispersion widened and targets lagged prices

The sector's price history over three years is a boom-bust cycle layered on a secular theme. 2024 was flat for uranium (URA -0.4%) but explosive for the AI-power generators (Vistra +262%, Talen about +212%) ([S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/market-intelligence/en/news-insights/articles/2025/1/independent-power-producer-shares-plunge-in-selloff-tied-to-ai-development-87276103)). 2025 delivered three policy-driven event rallies and one demand shock: the DeepSeek selloff of January 27, 2025 (Constellation -19 to -21%, Vistra -21 to -28%), the May 23 executive-order rally (Lightbridge +43%, NANO +30%, NuScale +20%) and the October 28-29 Westinghouse term sheet that sent Cameco up **23.4%** to a record ([CP24/Reuters](https://cp24.com/news/money/2025/05/23/nuclear-stocks-surge-on-report-of-trump-executive-orders-to-boost-industry); [Engineering News](https://www.engineeringnews.co.za/article/cameco-shares-surge-on-us-nuclear-partnership-2025-10-29)). Oklo returned 238% in 2025 ([Portfolioslab](https://portfolioslab.com/tools/stock-comparison/OKLO/VST)). Late October 2025 marked the sector top. The 2026 bear market then unfolded in stages: a dilution-driven slide in the SMR names, an inflation and rate shock as the Iran war pushed the Fed toward a hike (September CPI 3.7%, 10-year Treasury near 5.3%) ([Wisevoter](https://wisevoter.com/world/us/2026/11/01/nuclear-stocks-fall-rate-hike-fears)), an unwind of the AI-power trade, and on November 4 a post-midterm shock when a Democratic sweep in Texas, where the governor-elect campaigned on a stricter data-center moratorium, sent Vistra down **13.5%**, Constellation down 9% and GE Vernova down 9.6% in a day ([Finwire](https://finwire.io/news/stock-markets-news/vistra-plunges-135-as-texas-election-rattles-ai-power-trade-keybanc-downgrades); [GuruFocus](https://www.gurufocus.com/news/9171111/ge-vernova-gev-shares-fall-96-amid-midterm-election-concerns-on-november-4-2026)). KeyBanc downgraded Vistra to Sector Weight the same week, calling the result "a negative surprise" ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/keybanc-just-downgraded-vistra-stock-125003983.html)).

Oklo's roughly US$4.7 billion market capitalization now sits about 1.5 times its cash, meaning the market assigns only US$1.5-2 billion of enterprise value to its technology and 14 GW pipeline versus a double-digit-billion valuation a year earlier. Among the profitable names, the spread from Talen at about 10 times forward earnings to GE Vernova at about 51 times is wide enough that "nuclear" is not a single valuation regime. Sell-side consensus remains "Buy" or "Moderate Buy" on nearly every name because targets have lagged prices: Oklo's consensus of **US$55-68** versus a US$24 price, Cameco US$140-148 versus US$70, Vistra US$200 versus US$133, GE Vernova US$1,183 versus about US$790 ([StockAnalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/oklo/); [MarketBeat](https://www.marketbeat.com/instant-alerts/price-vistra-nyse-vst-stock-price-down-12-whats-next-2026-11-13/); [StockAnalysis GEV](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/gev/forecast/)). Implied upside of 50-180% usually signals stale targets rather than conviction, and the direction of travel is uniformly down: Wedbush cut Oklo from US$110 to US$36 while keeping Outperform and arguing the group trades "as if few or no reactors get built" ([Blockchain.News](https://blockchain.news/news/20261107-price-prediction-target-oklo-wedbush-cuts-as-derivatives-stay-mixed)); Citi, RBC and Goldman cut NuScale to US$3.50-5.00 on November 6 and Wedbush initiated it at Underperform because it "has yet to secure a binding order backed by financing" ([Stocktwits](https://stocktwits.com/news-articles/markets/equity/oklo-nne-smr-stocks-wedbush-picks-oklo-nano-nuclear-ai-power-demand-fuels-bull-case-warns-on-nu-scale/cNGQycVR95W)); Truist initiated Oklo, NuScale and NANO all at Hold ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/08/27/oklo-and-nuscale-power-have-shed-a-combined-853-th/)); GLJ Research initiated GE Vernova at Sell with a US$470 target ([StockAnalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/stocks/gev/ratings/)); JPMorgan trimmed BWXT to US$190 from US$230 ([Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/quote/BWXT/price-targets)). On the constructive side, Morgan Stanley is Overweight Constellation with a US$378 target ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/energy/articles/nuclear-energy-analyst-roundup-wall-015948632.html)), and Goldman added SMRs to its uranium model for the first time in May 2026, projecting about 46 GW of cumulative SMR deployment by 2045 adding roughly 62 Mlb of uranium demand ([Foreign Policy Journal](https://www.foreignpolicyjournal.com/2026/05/16/goldman-sachs-adds-small-modular-reactors-to-nuclear-model-projects-17-upside-to-uranium-demand-through-2045/)).

## The bull case rests on divergence, the bear case on dilution and time

The strongest bull argument in November 2026 is the commodity-equity divergence. Sprott's framing is that Kazatomprom has reset its 2026 output to about 77 Mlb with explicit intent to operate below capacity and may flex down a further 20%, that mine development is slow and concentrated, and that "the timing is particularly acute in 2026, as current decisions will shape early-2030s supply" ([Northern Miner](https://www.northernminer.com/news/uranium-market-gathers-momentum-in-2026-sprott/1003887637/)). With term prices at records and physical trusts at a 15% discount to NAV, bulls argue the setup historically resolves through equities re-rating or trust arbitrage buying rather than commodity collapse. For the operators, the bull case is fundamental and already visible in results: Constellation's Calpine deal lifted Q3 earnings, Talen guides to US$2.5-2.85 billion of 2027 EBITDA while retiring stock, and 45U supports existing-fleet cash flows through at least 2031. For the SMR names, bulls like Wedbush and HC Wainwright (Buy, US$90 on Oklo) argue AI power demand is intact and that the group is priced for failure ([MarketBeat](https://www.marketbeat.com/instant-alerts/analyst-truist-financial-issues-pessimistic-forecast-for-oklo-nyse-oklo-stock-price-2026-11-13/)); ARK Invest bought Oklo and X-energy on consecutive days in October 2026 with both down more than 60% year-to-date, one of the few visible named institutional bids ([Stocktwits](https://stocktwits.com/news-articles/markets/equity/cathie-wood-s-ark-invest-adds-these-nuclear-names-for-a-second-straight-day-even-as-their-shares-have-plummeted-more-than-60-in-2026/cZxvUg5RBwO)). Bulls also point out that the developers' balance sheets now dwarf their burn: Oklo's US$3.1 billion against US$120-150 million of annual cash use, X-energy's US$1.8 billion against roughly US$200 million, and NuScale's US$1.9 billion against about US$260 million of annualized operating expense remove near-term financing risk entirely.

The bear case has largely played out in price, and its most durable element is capital structure rather than technology. Kerrisdale Capital's November 2024 short thesis on Oklo argued the company would need about **US$2.7 billion** of additional capital over five years and called its 2027 deployment target "beyond optimistic" ([Kerrisdale](https://www.kerrisdalecap.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/OKLO-Kerrisdale.pdf)); Oklo has since raised more than that through ATMs, vindicating the dilution call even as the capital proved available. The reflexive loop is the problem: Oklo launched its second US$1 billion ATM in September 2026 with the stock already roughly 50% off ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/09/30/oklo-just-launched-another-1-billion-stock-sale-is/)), NuScale's US$750 million ATM equaled about 18% of its market cap in August ([FinanceFeeds](https://financefeeds.com/smr-stock-25-bull-case-7-bear-case/)), and lower prices mean more dilution per dollar raised. Short sellers booked roughly **US$2 billion** of profits on NuScale, Oklo and NANO in the year to August 2026 ([Seeking Alpha](https://seekingalpha.com/news/4634594-short-sellers-have-reaped-billions-in-profits-from-betting-against-small-modular-reactor)), and short interest remains at 18-22% of Oklo's float and nearly 30% of NANO's shares ([Seeking Alpha](https://seekingalpha.com/symbol/OKLO); [nuclear-news/FT](https://nuclear-news.net/2026/08/21/2-a-short-sellers-reap-2bn-profit-as-modular-nuclear-reactor-stocks-tumble/)), which makes the group prone to violent squeezes on any binding-order headline but also means ATM issuance supplies stock into every rally. Insider behavior is a signal bulls must explain: Oklo's co-founders each sold 40,000 shares at US$24.64 on November 4, 2026, roughly 80% below the 52-week high, after late-2025 insider sales "well over US$100 million" ([Seeking Alpha](https://seekingalpha.com/symbol/OKLO); [Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/08/27/oklo-and-nuscale-power-have-shed-a-combined-853-th/)).

Beyond capital structure, the bears' second pillar is the timeline mismatch. Hyperscaler SMR capacity is 2030-plus, US large-reactor first power is unlikely before the mid-2030s given Vogtle's roughly **US$36.8 billion, 11-year** precedent ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vogtle_Electric_Generating_Plant)), and Bank of America does not see real-world SMR adoption ticking up until 2030 at the earliest ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/08/27/oklo-and-nuscale-power-have-shed-a-combined-853-th/)), while data-center load forecasts peak in the 2027-2030 window. Every SMR business case depends on factory manufacturing cutting unit costs, "a thesis yet to be proven at commercial scale," and Darlington's overrun is the first real-world test result. MIT estimates the next AP1000 at US$8,300-10,375/kW overnight cost, converging toward about US$4,625/kW only after 10-20 units ([MIT CANES](https://web.mit.edu/kshirvan/www/research/ANP193%20TR%20CANES.pdf)).

The key risks cut across all four tiers. For operators, the Texas election and Democratic control of Georgia's Public Service Commission introduced state-level political risk to the data-center demand thesis for the first time, PJM caps limit capacity upside while the market monitor questions load forecasts, and restart slippage (Palisades, Crane's grid connection, Duane Arnold to 2029) defers contracted revenue. For miners, the record term price is not reaching income statements because of legacy contracts and operational shortfalls, developers face a closed equity window, and Kazakhstan's acid problem plus Russian retaliation could constrain supply in ways that help prices but hurt specific producers. For the fuel cycle, the January 1, 2028 waiver expiry arrives before any new Western enrichment capacity, creating a binary for Centrus and for any utility still dependent on TENEX. For developers, the Darlington overrun, the absence of a single priced binding order, Dow's 2027 decision on Long Mott, NuScale's TVA/ENTRA1 PPA (hoped for by end-2026 but explicitly not an order) and the DOE-to-NRC licensing transition for Oklo are the catalysts on which the next re-rating, up or down, depends. And across the board, rate sensitivity is the dominant macro driver: multiple 10-15% weekly drops in 2026 coincided with inflation prints and yield spikes rather than company news ([Foreign Policy Journal](https://www.foreignpolicyjournal.com/2026/10/21/oklo-nyse-oklo-and-nuscale-nyse-smr-tumble-15-as-rising-treasury-yields-hammer-nuclear-stocks/)).

## Conclusion

The 2026 drawdown has done something useful for anyone trying to understand this sector: it has pulled apart four businesses that the 2024-25 rally treated as one. Plant operators are now valued as power companies with contracted cash flows and political exposure, not as AI proxies; uranium miners trade as commodity producers whose realized prices lag the headline by US$20-25/lb; suppliers trade as industrials with backlogs; and SMR developers trade roughly at cash plus a modest option premium. That dispersion is a more honest map of where nuclear revenue actually exists today than the correlated surge of 2025. The one shared vulnerability is that the whole theme now depends on data-center load that two independent authorities, PJM's market monitor and Grid Strategies, say is overstated, and on a federal policy framework whose large-reactor commitments remain conditional or non-binding eighteen months after the executive orders.

The next twelve months will test a specific proposition: that regulatory approval was never the bottleneck. The NRC has delivered permits in 14 months and ahead of schedule, DOE has four test reactors through criticality, and Part 53 is law. What has not happened anywhere in the West is a utility or hyperscaler signing a priced, binding contract to buy a reactor. If one lands, whether a NuScale module order after a TVA PPA, Dow's investment decision at Long Mott, or the first financed AP1000 under the US$17.5 billion facility, the evidence from 2025's event rallies and today's 18-30% short interest suggests the repricing will be abrupt. If none does by late 2027, the Darlington overrun and the 2028 Russian fuel cliff will define the narrative, and the market's current verdict that the operators and suppliers, not the developers, are the investable expression of the nuclear theme will have been confirmed.
